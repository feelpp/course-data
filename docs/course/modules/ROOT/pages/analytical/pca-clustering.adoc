PCA finds orthogonal directions of maximum centred variance through SVD. Components depend on scale, sample, and measurement process; explained variance is not automatically scientific importance.

K-means minimises within-cluster squared Euclidean distance and favours roughly spherical, similarly scaled groups. Hierarchical linkage encodes another geometry; DBSCAN identifies density-connected regions and noise but depends strongly on scale and neighbourhood parameters.

== Principal component analysis

Let stem:[X\in\mathbb R^{n\times p}] be column-centred. Its singular-value decomposition is

[stem]
++++
X=U\Sigma V^\top,
\qquad
\Sigma=\operatorname{diag}(\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_r),
\quad \sigma_1\ge\cdots\ge\sigma_r>0.
++++

.Definition: Principal directions and scores
[.def#def:pca]
****
The columns stem:[v_j] of stem:[V] are principal directions. The score of observation stem:[i] on component stem:[j] is stem:[z_{ij}=x_i^\top v_j]. The sample variance explained by component stem:[j] is

[stem]
++++
\lambda_j=\frac{\sigma_j^2}{n-1},
\qquad
\operatorname{EVR}_j=\frac{\lambda_j}{\sum_{\ell=1}^{r}\lambda_\ell}.
++++
****

.Theorem: Best rank-q reconstruction
[.thm#thm:eckart-young]
****
The truncated SVD stem:[X_q=U_q\Sigma_qV_q^\top] minimises stem:[\lVert X-A\rVert_F] over matrices stem:[A] of rank at most stem:[q], and

[stem]
++++
\lVert X-X_q\rVert_F^2=\sum_{j=q+1}^{r}\sigma_j^2.
++++

This is an approximation result in squared Euclidean geometry, not a guarantee that the retained components preserve a scientific target.
****

Standardisation replaces the covariance geometry by correlation-like geometry. It is appropriate when differing units should not determine variance dominance, but it also gives a noisy low-variance measurement the same marginal scale as a precise one.

== Clustering objectives

.Definition: K-means objective
[.def#def:kmeans]
****
For assignments stem:[c_i\in\{1,\ldots,K\}] and centroids stem:[\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_K], K-means minimises

[stem]
++++
J(c,\mu)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lVert x_i-\mu_{c_i}\rVert_2^2.
++++

Alternating assignment and centroid updates decrease stem:[J] but can converge to different local minima.
****

DBSCAN instead defines a neighbourhood stem:[N_\varepsilon(x)=\{x_i:\lVert x_i-x\rVert\le\varepsilon\}] and connects points whose neighbourhood contains at least `min_samples` observations. It can represent non-convex clusters but struggles when densities vary strongly.

== Error estimation and stability

PCA directions and cluster assignments are sample-dependent estimators. Refit across bootstrap samples or meaningful groups. Align PCA signs/directions before comparison; compare subspaces when eigenvalues are close because individual directions may rotate without changing the spanned space.

For clustering, record objective variation, assignment agreement, and sensitivity to scaling, seed, stem:[K], distance, and algorithm. A stability score can compare pairs of co-clustered observations across fits, but high stability can still describe a stable artefact of sampling or preprocessing.

Internal scores such as silhouette use the same geometry that defined the clusters. They do not prove external usefulness. If labels are revealed post hoc, keep them entirely outside fitting and state that agreement is evaluation, not discovery.

== Worked example

.Example: Diagonal covariance
[.examp#ex:pca-diagonal]
****
Suppose a centred two-feature sample has covariance

[stem]
++++
S=\begin{bmatrix}9&0\\0&1\end{bmatrix}.
++++

The principal directions are the coordinate axes, with explained-variance ratios stem:[9/10] and stem:[1/10]. Retaining one component minimises squared reconstruction error, but if the second feature is a precisely measured safety variable, discarding it may still be scientifically unacceptable.
****
