Cross-validation estimates a selection procedure only when each fold reproduces the complete fitted workflow. Imputation, scaling, feature construction/selection, PCA, and threshold tuning belong inside the folds.

== Risk-estimation setting

Let stem:[\mathcal A_\lambda] denote a complete fitting algorithm with hyperparameter stem:[\lambda]. Given training data stem:[\mathcal D], it returns stem:[\widehat f_{\lambda,\mathcal D}]. The target is the risk of the *procedure*, not an abstract model detached from its fitted preprocessing.

.Definition: K-fold cross-validation estimate
[.def#def:kfold]
****
Partition indices into validation folds stem:[I_1,\ldots,I_K]. With stem:[I_{-k}] denoting all folds except stem:[k],

[stem]
++++
\widehat R_{\mathrm{CV}}(\lambda)
=\frac1K\sum_{k=1}^{K}
\frac{1}{|I_k|}\sum_{i\in I_k}
L\!\left(Y_i,
\mathcal A_\lambda(\mathcal D_{I_{-k}})(X_i)\right).
++++

Every learned operation is refitted using stem:[I_{-k}] only.
****

The selected hyperparameter is stem:[\widehat\lambda=\arg\min_\lambda\widehat R_{\mathrm{CV}}(\lambda)]. Reporting the same minimum as if stem:[\lambda] had been fixed in advance ignores selection optimism.

== Nested evaluation

Nested cross-validation separates selection from evaluation. In outer fold stem:[b], choose stem:[\widehat\lambda_b] using only the outer-training observations, refit there, and score once on outer-test fold stem:[J_b]:

[stem]
++++
\widehat R_{\mathrm{nested}}
=\frac1B\sum_{b=1}^{B}
\frac1{|J_b|}\sum_{i\in J_b}
L\!\left(Y_i,
\mathcal A_{\widehat\lambda_b}(\mathcal D_{J_{-b}})(X_i)\right).
++++

This estimates the combined data-dependent selection and fitting procedure for training sets of the outer-fold size.

== Choose folds from dependence

Use stratification for class prevalence, groups when related observations must remain together, and forward/blocked time splits when future information is unavailable. Random row folds are not a neutral default for repeated entities or autocorrelated measurements.

Grid search is appropriate for a small structured space; random search explores more dimensions under a fixed budget. Record distributions across folds, not only the best mean. Nested CV is required when the outer estimate must include the uncertainty introduced by model/hyperparameter selection.

== Error estimation

There are at least three sources of variation: which observations enter training, which enter validation, and which candidates are searched. Fold scores share training observations and are correlated. Their sample standard deviation is a useful stability diagnostic but stem:[s_{\mathrm{fold}}/\sqrt K] is not automatically a valid standard error.

Use repeated group/time-respecting outer splits, or a block/group bootstrap of the complete selection procedure, when uncertainty matters. Preserve candidate-level scores: if several candidates differ by less than the observed resampling variation, prefer the simpler or more stable choice rather than declaring a decisive winner.

Searching stem:[M] noisy candidates increases the chance that one looks good by luck. This multiple-comparisons effect is another reason to fix the search budget and use untouched outer or final evidence.

== Worked example: group leakage changes the estimated risk

[.worked-example-context#worked-model-selection-context]
=== Context

*Importance:* Essential (P0)

A predictive system will be applied to machines that were not represented during training. Determine whether ordinary random-row cross-validation or group-held-out validation estimates that deployment risk when every machine contributes repeated rows.

[.worked-example-method#worked-model-selection-method]
=== Method

*Importance:* Core (P1)

.Example: Group leakage
[.examp#ex:group-leakage]
****
Suppose 30 machines each contribute 20 rows and the target is nearly constant within a machine. Random five-fold CV places rows from every machine in training and validation, allowing a machine identifier or proxy to predict the label. `GroupKFold` holds out complete machines and estimates transfer to unseen machines. Both procedures may be coded correctly, but they estimate different risks.

With 600 rows, random folds have about 120 validation rows but nearly zero new-machine information. The effective sampling unit for the intended deployment is the 30 machines, not the 600 rows.

Numerically, row-wise five-fold validation reports averages of the form stem:[\widehat R_{\mathrm{row}}=5^{-1}\sum_{k=1}^5 R_k] over about stem:[600/5=120] rows per fold, whereas group validation holds out stem:[30/5=6] complete machines per fold. The formulas look alike; the validation units and target risks differ.
****

.Mathematical insight — the resampling unit defines the target risk
[IMPORTANT]
====
[.notebook-insight-label]
*Mathematical insight.*

*Core (P1).* Cross-validation is an estimator only after the data-generating and deployment units are defined. Random-row folds estimate performance on new rows from represented groups; `GroupKFold` estimates transfer to held-out groups. More rows within the same 30 machines improve within-machine precision but do not create 600 independent machines.
====

[.worked-example-implementation#worked-model-selection-implementation]
=== Implementation

*Importance:* Essential (P0)

The numerical unit in the problem is 30 machines, not 600 exchangeable rows. The executable verification creates grouped observations with a declared group-specific shortcut and compares stratified row-wise CV with `GroupKFold`. Its table reports all five fold scores, their mean and spread, and the validation unit; the paired plot makes the estimand change visible rather than hiding it behind one average.

[#fold-design-table]
[%dynamic%open,python,fail-on-error=,output=table,table-caption="Random-row and group-held-out cross-validation scores",table-description="Rows compare stratified random folds and GroupKFold. Columns report five fold accuracies, their mean and spread, and the effective validation unit."]
----
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
from sklearn.compose import ColumnTransformer
from sklearn.linear_model import LogisticRegression
from sklearn.model_selection import GroupKFold, StratifiedKFold, cross_val_score
from sklearn.pipeline import Pipeline
from sklearn.preprocessing import OneHotEncoder, StandardScaler

# Each group has a nearly constant label. Treating rows as independent leaks group identity.
rng = np.random.default_rng(20260722)
groups = np.repeat(np.arange(30), 20)
group_target = rng.integers(0, 2, size=30)
y = group_target[groups]
frame = pd.DataFrame(
    {
        "group": groups.astype(str),
        "weak_signal": y + rng.normal(scale=2.5, size=len(y)),
    }
)
preprocess = ColumnTransformer(
    [
        ("group", OneHotEncoder(handle_unknown="ignore"), ["group"]),
        ("signal", StandardScaler(), ["weak_signal"]),
    ]
)
model = Pipeline(
    [("preprocess", preprocess), ("model", LogisticRegression(max_iter=1000))]
)
random_cv = StratifiedKFold(5, shuffle=True, random_state=20260722)
group_cv = GroupKFold(5)
random_scores = cross_val_score(model, frame, y, cv=random_cv, scoring="accuracy", n_jobs=1)
group_scores = cross_val_score(
    model, frame, y, groups=groups, cv=group_cv, scoring="accuracy", n_jobs=1
)
assert random_scores.mean() > group_scores.mean() + 0.15

fold_summary = pd.DataFrame(
    [
        [*random_scores, random_scores.mean(), random_scores.std(), "rows"],
        [*group_scores, group_scores.mean(), group_scores.std(), "complete groups"],
    ],
    index=["stratified random rows", "GroupKFold"],
    columns=["fold 1", "fold 2", "fold 3", "fold 4", "fold 5", "mean", "spread", "unit"],
)
fold_summary
----

fold_index = np.arange(1, 6)
plt.style.use("tableau-colorblind10")
fig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=(7.5, 4.2), constrained_layout=True)

ax.plot(fold_index + 0.05, group_scores, "s--", label="GroupKFold")

=== Results

*Importance:* Essential (P0)

The random-row mean accuracy exceeds the group-held-out mean by more than stem:[0.15] in the controlled example. Each procedure reports five fold scores, but the random folds validate on rows from known machines while the group folds validate on six complete machines at a time. The paired figure shows that the difference is systematic across folds rather than caused by one isolated split.

[.worked-example-conclusion#worked-model-selection-conclusion]
=== Conclusion

*Importance:* Essential (P0)

For deployment to unseen machines, group-held-out validation is the relevant evidence; random-row validation answers a different and easier question. The synthetic shortcut demonstrates the leakage mechanism but does not quantify leakage in a real factory. With only 30 groups, uncertainty across possible held-out machine sets remains material, and model selection would require an outer group-respecting evaluation.
