== 1. Ktirio Urban Building: what are we averaging?

A district model connects building geometry, materials, weather, and energy or comfort results. Building identifiers must match across tables. Power in kW, energy in kWh, and energy per floor area answer different questions.

Consider two *synthetic* buildings:

[cols="1,2,2,2",options="header"]
|===
|Building |Annual energy |Floor area |Energy per area
|A |1,000 kWh |10 m² |100 kWh/m²
|B |1,000 kWh |100 m² |10 kWh/m²
|===

*Predict before calculating:* what is the district's energy intensity? Discuss which building should receive more weight, and why.

[%collapsible]
.What the two calculations mean
====
The mean building intensity is `(100 + 10) / 2 = 55 kWh/m²`: it weights buildings equally. District energy divided by district area is `2000 / 110 ≈ 18.18 kWh/m²`: it weights square metres equally. Choose the question before choosing the weights.

[source,python]
----
energy_kwh = [1000, 1000]
area_m2 = [10, 100]
mean_building = sum(e / a for e, a in zip(energy_kwh, area_m2)) / 2
district = sum(energy_kwh) / sum(area_m2)
assert abs(mean_building - 55.0) < 1e-10
assert abs(district - 2000 / 110) < 1e-10
print(f"Equal buildings: {mean_building:.2f}; equal area: {district:.2f} kWh/m²")
----
====

*Connection:* Database gives us keys and joins; Scientific Computing produces physical predictions; HPC makes larger calculations feasible; this course checks what comparison the resulting table supports.

Project context: https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.14535[the Ktirio framework paper].

== 2. Sepsis: what was known at prediction time?

A patient timeline contains irregular observations and missing measurements. A predictive task must state its observation window and future target window. Split patients or time periods according to the intended use before fitting imputation or a model.

*Discuss:* if windows from one patient appear in both training and testing, does the score establish performance for a new patient? What if a column was recorded only after the outcome?

*Connection:* Random Models helps describe variability; this course builds an honest baseline and evaluation; SciML can extend the model later. A synthetic classroom result does not establish clinical usefulness.

== 3. Organ on Chip with Italy: from images to an observable

A microscopy sequence can become cell positions, trajectories, velocities, density, or flux. Pixel size and time intervals determine physical units. Tracking choices affect the quantity compared with a mathematical model.

*Discuss:* if a tracker switches a cell's identity between frames, what happens to its estimated velocity? Are neighbouring frames independent experimental replications?

*Connection:* image processing extracts observations; statistics characterises their uncertainty; Scientific Computing and PDE modelling predict observables for comparison. In this course, make the image-to-table transformation and experimental unit explicit.

== 4. JNL-G with LNCMI: align before comparing

A magnet/facility digital twin connects multiphysics models with electrical and cooling observations. Timestamps, sensor units, and operating regimes must agree before a model–measurement discrepancy can be interpreted.

*Discuss:* power readings are recorded at unequal time intervals. Is their arithmetic mean sufficient to compute energy? State the interpolation assumption you would need between readings.

*Connection:* Scientific Computing and reduced models produce predictions; data processing aligns them with observations; HPC, SciML, and Optimization support larger models and future decisions.

Project context: https://anr.fr/Projet-ANR-25-CE46-1157[ANR JNL-G description].
