== The transition: why principal components?

The covariance matrix S and correlation matrix R describe pairs of original
variables. PCA asks a new question: can combinations of those variables
represent observations with fewer coordinates while retaining much of their
variance? Projection defines scores; a unit-length constraint makes the
variance maximization meaningful; eigenvectors solve it and SVD computes it.
A principal axis need not represent a directly measured physical quantity.
Its coefficients and the original observations support its interpretation.

The xref:sessions/pca-practical.adoc[dedicated practical] reuses Beijing data
and the existing worked demonstrations. It supplies data loading and plot
layouts while students calculate directions, scores, retained variance and
reconstruction error, with three checkpoints and a common comparison of two
and three components. It occupies 45 minutes of the two-hour November class,
not an additional contact session.

== Data and scope

The unchanged canonical Beijing table contains 1,008 station-hours from
Aotizhongxin, Changping and Dingling, 1–14 March 2013. The matrix and PCA
examples use the same 1,002 complete rows for six variables. The separate
xref:attachment$datasets/air-quality-seasonal/DATASET.md[licensed seasonal supplement]
is optional; its March rows repeat the canonical slice and must not be
concatenated as additional observations. All required data are in the bundle.

PCA is descriptive here. A later predictive transformation must be fitted
only on training data. The supplied regression estimates same-hour PM2.5
from available same-hour measurements, not unknown future covariates. The
already explored extract cannot provide a pristine final test.
