Exploration is a disciplined search for structure, quality problems, assumptions, and alternative explanations. It is not permission to tune repeatedly on final-test data or to present attractive plots without a population claim.

== Question before figure

For every figure, write the question, population/sample, unit, grouping, scale, missing-data rule, and uncertainty or variation shown. Label axes with units. Avoid truncated axes for magnitude comparisons, overplotting without density/alpha/bin decisions, dual axes that imply a false relationship, and colour palettes that hide categories.

== Sampling and representativeness

The Beijing slice contains three stations and fourteen days from March 2013. It can support exercises about those recorded observations; it cannot represent all Beijing stations, seasons, current air quality, Strasbourg, or causal effects of weather. Temporal autocorrelation means hourly rows are not automatically independent. A random row split can exaggerate forecasting evidence by placing near-neighbour times in both train and test.

== Laboratory

Audit missingness and coverage, plot station/time distributions, compare within- and between-station variation, and write one supported and one unsupported claim.

== Independent evidence brief

Produce one page with exactly three figures:

. coverage/missingness over time or station;
. a distribution showing scale and extremes;
. a relationship stratified by a relevant group or time unit.

Each caption must state what is shown, the observation count after missing-data handling, and one limitation. Include a paragraph defining the target population and explaining whether the sample can support it.

== Acceptance criteria

The brief is reproducible, figures have units and readable labels, missing data are not silently discarded, aggregation matches the declared unit, and claims remain descriptive unless an identification design supports more.
